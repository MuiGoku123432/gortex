## 1. System Classification

**System Type:** Bounded structured extraction with deterministic validation and human-in-the-loop review

**Description:**
An explicitly authorized user submits a Phase 7 bounded, redacted context manifest to an approved model through Gortex's existing Go provider/service abstraction. The model may propose closed-schema assertions about that evidence. Gortex, not the model, validates schema, scope, evidence existence, evidence activity, source ranges, assertion vocabulary, and version lineage before appending an `AI_INFERRED` claim to SQLite. Authorized reviewers append confirm, contradict, reject, or supersede events. Claims and reviews never mutate deterministic graph facts, never close deterministic findings, and never become dependencies of indexing or deterministic queries. Good behavior is auditable, reproducible, fail-closed, idempotent, and exactly neutral to the canonical deterministic subgraph.

**Critical Failure Modes:**
1. An output cites an unknown, inactive, stale, or out-of-scope graph ID/source range and is persisted anyway.
2. A claim or review mutates deterministic nodes, edges, findings, identity, or lifecycle state, including indirect mutation through a trusted-view projection.
3. Retries create duplicate active claims, or a new model/prompt/context/schema/evidence version silently overwrites prior lineage.
4. Unreviewed, contradicted, rejected, stale, or superseded claims enter deterministic or trusted views.
5. Conflicting claims collapse through last-write-wins behavior, or review history loses actor, time, rationale, originating claim, or model provenance.
6. AI-enabled and AI-disabled runs produce different canonical active deterministic subgraphs.

### Authority Boundaries

| Record | Producer | Authority | Mutation model |
|--------|----------|-----------|----------------|
| Deterministic node/edge/finding | Parser, extractor, resolver | Deterministic graph truth | Existing source-owner reconciliation |
| AI claim | Validated provider output | Proposal only | Append-only |
| Human review event | Authorized reviewer | Review judgment, not parser truth | Append-only |
| Claim eligibility/current state | Deterministic projection over claims, reviews, and evidence generations | Query convenience | Rebuildable; never authoritative history |

## 1b. Domain Context

**Industry Vertical:** Developer tooling for regulated or high-consequence mainframe modernization

**User Population:** Mainframe engineers, COBOL maintainers, modernization architects, code-intelligence operators, security reviewers, and designated claim reviewers working with incomplete proprietary estates.

**Stakes Level:** High

**Output Consequence:** Claims may influence impact analysis, migration sequencing, parser/extractor priorities, and human understanding of legacy behavior. A false or stale claim can misdirect modernization work, conceal an unresolved boundary, or leak cross-repository knowledge. Therefore a claim is advisory until reviewed and is never deterministic truth.

### What Domain Experts Evaluate Against

| Dimension | Good (expert accepts) | Bad (expert flags) | Stakes | Source |
|-----------|-----------------------|--------------------|--------|--------|
| Evidence sufficiency | Every material assertion cites exact, active evidence that a reviewer can retrieve; omissions are declared | Rationale relies on uncited source, inaccessible artifacts, model memory, or name similarity | Critical | Requirements CLAIM-04/06; threat T6/T7 |
| Epistemic honesty | Assertion distinguishes observed facts, interpretation, unresolved boundaries, and missing context | Claim presents an unavailable copybook, dynamic call target, or parser recovery as known fact | Critical | Product principle; GRAPH-SCHEMA governing rules |
| Mainframe semantic precision | Uses supported COBOL/DB2/CICS/IDMS vocabulary and preserves program, library, schema, region, and repository qualifiers | Collapses same-named programs/resources or treats SQL aliases/CTEs as physical tables | Critical | GRAPH-SCHEMA concept mapping and scope contract |
| Provenance reproducibility | Provider, model, prompt, context builder, schema, policy, parser/extractor, source revision, and evidence digest identify the exact basis | Versions are missing, mutable labels are used, or current evidence is substituted for historical evidence | High | CLAIM-05; REVIEW-03/05 |
| Missing-context discipline | Names unavailable artifacts and omitted evidence that could materially change the assertion | Empty `missing_context` is used despite unresolved findings or bounded context omissions | High | AI-07; CLAIM-03 |
| Review decision quality | Reviewer checks cited evidence, records a domain rationale, and uses supersession only with an explicit replacement | Review is based solely on confidence, deletes history, or confirms beyond the evidence/version scope | High | REVIEW-01 through REVIEW-06 |
| Query trust labeling | Results clearly distinguish deterministic, unresolved, AI-inferred, human-confirmed, contradicted, stale, rejected, and superseded outcomes | Mixed results omit evidence class/lifecycle or imply confirmed means deterministic | Critical | QUERY-11 |

### Known Failure Modes in This Domain

- Same-named programs, copybooks, DB2 tables, CICS resources, or IDMS records from another scope are cited as support.
- A parser `ERROR`/`MISSING` region is treated as absent source, or an unavailable artifact is treated as parsed evidence.
- A model invents copybook contents, a dynamic call target, business meaning, or a physical table behind an alias.
- A source/parser/extractor change leaves a formerly reviewed claim eligible even though its evidence generation changed.
- High model confidence is treated as evidence or automatic promotion.
- Human confirmation is interpreted as permission to rewrite a parser fact or close a deterministic finding.
- A later claim overwrites a contradictory earlier claim instead of preserving both assertions and their evidence.
- Prompt injection in source comments or identifiers attempts to widen scope or alter output semantics.

### Regulatory / Compliance Context

No single domain regulation is assumed. Organizational source-classification, data residency, provider authorization, retention, audit, legal-hold, and reviewer-authorization policies remain mandatory inputs from Phase 7. OWASP LLM sensitive-information and prompt-injection guidance applies. Provider availability is not provider authorization. This phase must not resolve organization-specific policy placeholders by guessing.

## 2. Framework Decision

**Selected Framework:** Existing Gortex native Go AI infrastructure (`internal/llm`, `internal/llm/provider`, and service/provider structured-output path) with authoritative SQLite claim/review storage

**Version:** Repository-pinned implementation at the Phase 8 implementation baseline; Go 1.27.0. Persist an explicit claim schema version (start at `claim.v1`) independently of application version. If Phase 7 promotes the already-indirect `jsonschema/v6` dependency, pin `v6.0.3`; otherwise use the Phase 7 accepted native validator. Do not add a second schema engine.

**Model Provider:** Policy-selected existing Gortex provider. No provider/model is authorized by this specification. Persist the resolved provider and model/deployment for every invocation; routing must not escape the authorized pair.

**Rationale:**
The workflow is one bounded model call followed by deterministic validation and append-only persistence, not a general agent graph. Gortex already supports local, hosted, CLI, and custom providers, structured JSON shapes, model identity, usage accounting, and scoped service execution. Reusing those seams satisfies AI-03, preserves Go deployment and provider neutrality, and keeps policy enforcement outside model behavior. SQLite already owns authoritative transactions and lifecycle. The hard problem is closed-schema and semantic admission, versioned provenance, immutable review history, view eligibility, and equality testing; an orchestration framework adds no value to those controls.

**Alternatives Considered:**

| Framework | Ruled Out Because |
|-----------|------------------|
| LangGraph | Strong human-in-loop/checkpointing features, but introduces Python/TypeScript runtime, duplicate state authority, and graph orchestration for a deliberately linear operation. SQLite events provide the required audit trail. |
| LangChain | General abstractions and integration surface exceed the narrow structured-output use case and duplicate existing providers. |
| LlamaIndex | Retrieval is deterministic graph/context selection from Phase 7, not vector/document RAG; adding an index risks a second evidence authority. |
| OpenAI Agents SDK | Provider lock-in and agent primitives conflict with provider-neutral policy and no-tool claim generation. |
| Anthropic Agent SDK | Claude lock-in and autonomous filesystem/tool capabilities expand the trust boundary unnecessarily. |
| CrewAI/AutoGen/Google ADK | Multi-agent delegation is not required and would make provenance, authorization, and deterministic replay harder. |
| New custom AI framework | Explicitly prohibited by the milestone and unnecessary because existing Go abstractions cover transport and structured output. |

**Vendor Lock-In Accepted:** No. Provider-specific structured-output modes may be used behind the existing abstraction, but the persisted `claim.v1` contract and semantic validator are provider-independent.

**Decision Rule:** If implementation planning discovers that the existing provider interface cannot request an arbitrary closed schema, extend that interface minimally. Do not route claim generation through the free-form `RunAgent` tool loop and do not add an orchestration dependency.

### Entry Point Pattern

```go
func GenerateClaims(ctx context.Context, req ClaimRequest) ([]Claim, error) {
    // Phase 7 boundary: authorize, scope, minimize, redact, and digest first.
    manifest, err := contextBuilder.Build(ctx, req)
    if err != nil {
        return nil, err
    }

    output, err := claimProvider.CompleteStructured(ctx, manifest, ClaimSchemaV1)
    if err != nil {
        return nil, err
    }

    candidates, err := validateClosedSchema(output)
    if err != nil {
        return nil, err // no persistence on structural failure
    }
    claims, err := validateEvidenceAndScope(ctx, manifest, candidates)
    if err != nil {
        return nil, err // no persistence on semantic failure
    }

    return claimStore.AppendValidated(ctx, claims) // idempotent stable IDs
}
```

### Key Abstractions

| Concept | What It Is | When You Use It |
|---------|------------|-----------------|
| Phase 7 context manifest | Immutable digest of authorized evidence, omissions, redactions, policy, and scope | Input to every provider call and semantic validator |
| `claim.v1` envelope | Closed provider-neutral output schema | Provider request and structural admission |
| Semantic validator | Deterministic resolver for evidence IDs/ranges, scope, activity, versions, and predicates | Before claim ID computation/persistence |
| Claim ledger | Append-only validated assertions and evidence links in SQLite | Audit and explicit claim queries |
| Review event ledger | Append-only reviewer decisions | Confirm, contradict, reject, or supersede |
| Eligibility projection | Rebuildable current status derived from immutable events and current evidence generations | Trusted and lifecycle-filtered queries |
| Canonical deterministic digest | Stable serialization/hash of active deterministic nodes, edges, and findings only | AI-on/off equality gate |

### Common Pitfalls

1. Provider-side JSON/schema mode improves generation but is not admission validation; always revalidate locally.
2. `additionalProperties: false` must apply recursively, not only at the top level.
3. JSON decoding into permissive maps or structs silently ignores unknown fields unless explicitly prohibited.
4. Evidence validity is snapshot-specific; existence alone is insufficient without matching scope, source revision, parser/extractor generation, and active status.
5. Timestamps, request IDs, response ordering, and database row IDs must not enter stable claim identity.
6. `RunAgent` currently permits graph tools and conversation recording; it is not the claim-write boundary without Phase 7 policy and metadata-only logging guarantees.
7. Human confirmation changes claim eligibility, not evidence class of deterministic records.
8. Staleness must be appended or deterministically derived; never erase or update historical claim/review rows in place.

### Recommended Project Structure

```text
internal/
  llm/                    # existing provider and structured-output seams
  claim/                  # schema, structural/semantic validation, stable identity
  graph/store_sqlite/     # authoritative claim/review transactions and migrations
  query/                  # explicit evidence/lifecycle view filters
  review/                 # authorization and append-only review command handling
```

Exact package placement is for implementation planning after reuse analysis. Avoid a parallel service or generic framework layer.

## 4. Implementation Guidance

### Model Configuration

- Provider/model/deployment: selected only by Phase 7 policy and persisted after routing resolution.
- Temperature: `0` or the provider's lowest supported deterministic setting.
- Tool access: none for the model call. Context selection occurs before dispatch; model-produced commands/tool calls are rejected.
- Output: one closed JSON object conforming to `claim.v1`; no prose wrapper.
- Limits: policy-bounded input bytes/tokens, maximum claims per response, evidence references per claim, assertion/rationale/missing-context lengths, and output tokens.
- Retry: at most one schema-repair retry if Phase 7 policy permits. Retry uses the same context/prompt/schema/model lineage and stable idempotency key. Semantic failures are not repaired by widening context automatically.

### Core Pattern

1. Load an immutable Phase 7 context manifest for one authorized scope and evidence generation.
2. Call an existing provider with no arbitrary tools and a provider-neutral closed schema.
3. Decode with unknown-field rejection and validate all bounds/enums.
4. Resolve every cited graph ID and exact source range against the manifest allow-set and current authoritative snapshot.
5. Reject the entire response transaction on any invalid candidate; do not partially persist a mixed response.
6. Canonicalize assertion and ordered evidence set, compute stable claim ID, and append with idempotent conflict handling.
7. Append review/lifecycle events separately. Build current status from ordered events plus evidence-generation validity.
8. Expose claims only through explicit evidence-class/lifecycle filters. The default deterministic view never joins the ledger.

### Closed Claim Schema (`claim.v1`)

The provider output contains only `schema_version`, `claims`, and the fields below. Every object uses recursive `additionalProperties: false`.

| Field | Constraint |
|-------|------------|
| `schema_version` | Exact enum `claim.v1` |
| `claims` | Array, bounded by policy, deterministic order not trusted |
| `assertion.kind` | Closed allow-list of Phase 8-supported predicates |
| `assertion.subject_id` | Required existing in-scope graph/finding ID |
| `assertion.object_id` or `assertion.value` | Exactly one, type/predicate constrained; object must be existing in-scope ID |
| `confidence` | Finite number in `[0,1]`; informational only |
| `rationale` | Required concise bounded string; no raw source requirement |
| `missing_context` | Required bounded array, allowed to be empty only when manifest has no material omissions |
| `evidence` | Non-empty bounded array of graph-ID or exact source-range references |

Provider/model/prompt/context/schema/policy versions are authoritative request metadata supplied by Gortex, not trusted fields copied from model output.

### Semantic Evidence Validation

All checks are deterministic and occur before persistence:

- The request scope is explicit and matches workspace, project, repository allow-set, and any mainframe resolution domain.
- Every graph ID exists in SQLite, is active in the manifest snapshot, belongs to the allow-set, and is present in the context manifest.
- Every source range names an authorized normalized path and revision, has valid ordered byte/row/column bounds, lies within the authorized file bytes, and matches a range admitted by the manifest.
- Subject/object kinds are valid for the assertion predicate. Unknown predicates and attempted deterministic lifecycle operations are rejected.
- Evidence supports the referenced subject/object structurally; a citation to an unrelated valid ID is rejected.
- `missing_context` is consistent with manifest omissions and unresolved evidence. The model cannot claim unavailable context was inspected.
- The response cannot set evidence class, lifecycle, reviewer identity, scope, provider lineage, or deterministic graph fields.
- Any stale manifest, changed evidence generation, parser/extractor version mismatch, or authorization change rejects admission and requires a new request.

### Stable Identity and Provenance

Compute `claim_id` from a canonical versioned tuple:

```text
claim.v1
+ policy scope identity
+ canonical assertion
+ sorted unique evidence-reference identities including evidence revisions
+ provider identity and resolved model/deployment
+ prompt-template version
+ context-builder version and context digest
+ output-schema version
```

Do not include generated timestamps, transport request IDs, response order, reviewer state, or database row IDs. A unique constraint on `claim_id` makes identical retries return the existing claim without appending a duplicate active claim. A changed model, prompt, context builder, schema, policy scope, assertion, or evidence revision creates a distinct claim with explicit lineage.

Persist, at minimum:

- Claim ID, schema version, normalized assertion, confidence, rationale, missing context, created time, and request/audit correlation ID.
- Workspace/project/repository scope and mainframe resolution domain where applicable.
- Evidence references and immutable evidence revision/generation identifiers.
- Provider, resolved model/deployment, prompt-template version, context-builder version/digest, output-schema version, and policy version.
- No raw prompt, source context, or raw provider response by default.

### Append-Only Claim and Review Events

- Validated claims are immutable `AI_INFERRED` records.
- Review actions append events with event ID, claim ID, reviewer identity, authorization/policy version, decision, timestamp, optional bounded rationale, and replacement/related claim ID where required.
- Allowed review decisions are `CONFIRM`, `CONTRADICT`, `REJECT`, and `SUPERSEDE`.
- `SUPERSEDE` requires an existing validated replacement claim and appends reciprocal lineage; it never edits either claim.
- `CONTRADICT` may point to one or more conflicting claims. Both remain queryable.
- Evidence invalidation appends or derives `STALE` from immutable generation changes; implementation planning must choose one authoritative mechanism and prove deterministic replay. In either case, history remains unchanged.
- Events use optimistic expected-version or transaction sequencing so concurrent reviewers cannot silently overwrite each other. Multiple non-exclusive judgments may coexist; invalid transitions fail explicitly.

### Lifecycle Projection

| Outcome | Meaning | Deterministic view | Trusted view | Audit view |
|---------|---------|--------------------|--------------|------------|
| `DETERMINISTIC` | Parser/extractor/resolver fact | Included | Included | Included |
| `PARSE_UNRESOLVED` / `EXTERNAL_UNRESOLVED` | Deterministic known gap | Included when requested | Included when requested | Included |
| `AI_INFERRED` pending | Validated but unreviewed claim | Excluded | Excluded | Included |
| `HUMAN_CONFIRMED` active | Confirmed claim with still-valid evidence/version basis | Excluded | Included only by explicit trusted-claims option | Included |
| `CONTRADICTED` | Claim has contradiction event | Excluded | Excluded | Included |
| `REJECTED` | Reviewer rejected claim | Excluded | Excluded | Included |
| `STALE` | Evidence or lineage basis no longer current | Excluded | Excluded | Included |
| `SUPERSEDED` | Replaced by a named successor claim | Excluded | Excluded | Included |

Default native queries remain deterministic-only. `trusted` is a distinct explicit view, not an alias for deterministic. Confidence never changes eligibility. Human-confirmed claims retain `AI_INFERRED` origin and model provenance.

### Conflicting Claims

Conflicts are first-class relations/events, not uniqueness violations. The ledger may contain multiple active pending or reviewed claims about the same subject/predicate. Queries return each claim with evidence and status. No claim writes assertion fields onto the domain entity. A reviewer may contradict one claim and confirm another, but both histories remain visible. Supersession expresses replacement lineage, not deletion or proof that every conflict is resolved.

### State Management

SQLite remains the sole authority for deterministic facts, claims, reviews, and current projection checkpoints. Claim/review writes use dedicated error-returning transactions, foreign keys to immutable claim IDs, unique idempotency keys, and no file-owner eviction semantics. Source reconciliation may invalidate eligibility but cannot delete ledger history. Any cached current-state table is rebuildable from claim/review events plus authoritative evidence generations.

### Context Window Strategy

Use only the Phase 7 context manifest: selected finding/range, containing hierarchy, relevant declarations/relationships, approved dependencies, explicit omissions, and missing artifacts. No vector retrieval, conversational memory, entire-repository fallback, or automatic scope expansion. Reject over-budget requests. Claim validation cites IDs/ranges, not model token offsets.

### Query Contract (`QUERY-11`)

Every claim-capable CLI/MCP response exposes evidence class, origin, lifecycle, scope, claim ID, evidence references, and lineage versions. Filters distinguish all required outcomes. Deterministic commands retain current behavior and exclude claims unless an explicit claim/trusted/audit option is selected. Unknown or future lifecycle values fail closed rather than joining trusted results.

### AI-On/Off Deterministic Equality

For identical final source, parser, extractor, retrieval, material configuration, and scope:

1. Run canonical indexing/query acceptance with every provider disabled.
2. Record a canonical sorted serialization and digest of active deterministic nodes, edges, and findings, excluding timestamps and AI ledger tables.
3. Enable authorized AI, generate/retry/review/conflict/stale claims, and rerun deterministic queries.
4. Require byte-identical canonical serialization/digest and equal deterministic query results.
5. Repeat after restart and incremental edit/delete/restore/parser-version sequences.

Any difference is a release blocker regardless of model quality.

## 4b. AI Systems Best Practices

### Structured Outputs in Go (Pydantic Gate Adaptation)

The generic GSD template asks for Pydantic. Pydantic is not applicable to this Go repository and must not be introduced. The equivalent closed-schema pattern is a strict Go DTO plus a versioned JSON Schema with recursive unknown-field rejection and a separate semantic validator.

```go
type ClaimOutputV1 struct {
    SchemaVersion string             `json:"schema_version"`
    Claims        []ClaimCandidateV1 `json:"claims"`
}

type ClaimCandidateV1 struct {
    Assertion       AssertionV1  `json:"assertion"`
    Confidence      float64      `json:"confidence"`
    Rationale       string       `json:"rationale"`
    MissingContext  []string     `json:"missing_context"`
    Evidence        []EvidenceV1 `json:"evidence"`
}

func decodeClaimOutputV1(raw []byte) (ClaimOutputV1, error) {
    dec := json.NewDecoder(bytes.NewReader(raw))
    dec.DisallowUnknownFields()
    // Decode one object, reject trailing tokens, validate claim.v1 schema,
    // then run scope/evidence/predicate validation before any write.
}
```

Provider-native schema enforcement is generation guidance only. Gortex performs local structural and semantic validation. On structural failure, record metadata-only failure and optionally perform one policy-approved repair retry. On semantic failure, reject without automatic context widening or persistence.

### Async-First Design

Use Go `context.Context` throughout for cancellation and deadlines. Provider I/O may be asynchronous internally, but claim admission is a linear transaction boundary. Never hold a SQLite write transaction open during provider calls. Streaming is unnecessary because partial output cannot be validated or persisted; await the complete bounded JSON response. Cancellation before commit produces no claim.

### Prompt Engineering Discipline

- System instructions define the closed assertion vocabulary, evidence-only rule, missing-context duty, and source-as-untrusted-data boundary.
- User content contains a delimited, redacted context manifest; repository text is data, never instructions.
- Few-shot examples include valid, abstaining, unknown-ID, missing-artifact, and conflicting-claim cases using synthetic data.
- Prompts never authorize scope, provider, tools, lifecycle, or writes.
- Prompt templates are immutable/versioned inputs to claim identity and staleness.

### Context Window Management

Context is selected deterministically before dispatch and identified by a manifest digest. Prefer omission with an explicit missing-context entry over truncation that changes semantics. Sort evidence deterministically, cap item and byte/token counts, and record omitted IDs/classes without raw omitted source. A larger context requires a new authorized manifest and therefore a new lineage basis.

### Cost and Latency Budget

Phase 7 policy supplies per-request input/output and retention limits. Phase 8 adds maximum claims/evidence references and one optional repair retry. Record usage, latency, and resolved model as metadata without raw content. No hidden subcalls, agent loops, judge calls, or automatic model routing outside the authorized model. Evaluation runs use approved fixtures and explicit budget approval.

## 5. Evaluation Strategy

### Dimensions

| Dimension | Rubric | Measurement Approach | Priority |
|-----------|--------|----------------------|----------|
| Closed-schema validity | Pass only if exactly `claim.v1`, all required fields/bounds/enums valid, recursive unknown fields rejected, and no trailing payload | Code | Critical |
| Evidence citation validity | Pass only if 100% of citations exist, are active, in manifest and scope, revision-exact, and structurally relevant | Code plus expert audit | Critical |
| Unsupported-claim admission | Pass only if zero unsupported, invented, unavailable-as-known, or unrelated-evidence claims are persisted | Code plus human | Critical |
| Scope isolation | Pass only if cross-workspace/project/repository/library canaries yield zero admitted claims and zero leaked metadata/content | Code/security canary | Critical |
| Deterministic equality | Pass only if AI-on/off canonical deterministic digests and native deterministic query results are exactly equal | Code | Critical |
| Retry idempotency | Pass only if identical retries return one claim ID and one immutable claim row | Code | Critical |
| Provenance completeness | Pass only if every admitted claim has all required provider/model/prompt/context/schema/policy/evidence versions | Code | Critical |
| Lifecycle correctness | Pass only if confirm/contradict/reject/supersede/stale transitions produce expected eligibility without history mutation | State-machine/property tests | Critical |
| Conflict preservation | Pass only if incompatible claims coexist and no domain entity or prior claim is overwritten | Code plus query golden | High |
| Domain semantic precision | 5: exact qualified semantics and honest uncertainty; 3: materially correct with non-critical imprecision; 1: false binding, invented artifact, or evidence-class confusion | Human rubric; optional calibrated judge for triage only | High |
| Missing-context quality | 5: all material omissions stated; 3: incomplete but does not change conclusion; 1: conceals unresolved or unavailable evidence | Human | High |
| Review quality | 5: decision cites evidence and version basis with clear rationale; 3: correct but weak rationale; 1: confidence-only or wrong-scope decision | Human | High |

### Acceptance Thresholds

- Structural schema validity of admitted claims: `100%`.
- Evidence/scope citation validity of admitted claims: `100%`.
- Unsupported-claim and protected-source leakage among admitted claims: `0`.
- AI-on/off canonical deterministic equality: exact, `100%` of runs.
- Identical-retry deduplication: `100%`.
- Provenance completeness and valid lifecycle projection: `100%`.
- Domain semantic precision: no score `1` admitted; mean at least `4.0/5` on the reference set before leaving experimental status.
- LLM judge, if later used for triage: at least `0.7` correlation with expert labels and never an admission or trust gate.

### Required Eval Matrix

- Providers: at least one approved provider plus provider-disabled path; schema behavior differences must not change local admission rules.

- Scopes: single repository, duplicate names across repositories/projects/workspaces, explicitly authorized cross-repository evidence, and denied ambiguous scope.
- Failures: malformed JSON, unknown fields, duplicate keys/IDs, oversized output, timeout/cancel, provider error, validation error, SQLite error, concurrent review, and stale manifest.

## 6. Guardrails

### Online (Real-Time)

| Guardrail | Trigger | Intervention |
|-----------|---------|--------------|
| Phase 7 authorization | Provider/model/scope/data-class/retention policy absent or mismatch | Block before context serialization |
| Closed-schema validation | Invalid JSON, unknown field, enum/bound/size/count failure, or trailing payload | Block; metadata-only failure; optional one repair retry |
| Semantic evidence validation | Unknown/inactive/out-of-manifest/out-of-scope/unrelated ID or invalid range | Block entire response; no partial persistence |
| Stale manifest/version | Evidence, source, parser/extractor, context builder, prompt, schema, model, or policy basis changed | Block and require a new authorized invocation |
| Mutation boundary | Attempt to set deterministic class/state, close finding, retarget edge, invent target, or invoke tools | Block and security-audit metadata |
| Trusted-view eligibility | Claim unreviewed, contradicted, rejected, stale, superseded, or unknown state | Exclude fail-closed |
| Review authorization | Reviewer or action not authorized for claim scope/policy | Block event append |
| Supersession integrity | Replacement claim missing, invalid, wrong scope, or self-referential | Block |
| Audit/storage health | Required claim/review audit transaction cannot commit | Fail closed; no success response |
| Context/output budget | Policy byte/token/item limits exceeded | Block; never silently truncate/widen |

### Offline (Flywheel)

| Metric | Sampling Strategy | Action on Degradation |
|--------|-------------------|----------------------|
| Schema and semantic rejection families | Review all new failure signatures; aggregate known signatures without raw source | Fix prompt/schema only if validator remains strict; add regression fixture |
| Human review outcome distribution | Stratify by provider/model, prompt/context/schema version, assertion kind, and gap family | Suspend weak versions; investigate evidence selection and deterministic extractor opportunities |
| Contradiction/staleness rate | Oversample conflicts and claims invalidated soon after creation | Improve context/version contract; never auto-promote |
| Unsupported-claim rate | Expert review all admitted experimental claims initially, then risk-weighted sampling | Stop exposure beyond audit, fix, and rerun full reference set |
| Gap-family frequency and graph impact | Aggregate reviewed findings by deterministic class/reason and affected programs | Propose parser/extractor work; AI claims never close the gap |
| AI-on/off equality | Every acceptance build and lifecycle sequence | Release block on any difference |

## 7. Production Monitoring

**Tracing Tool:** Existing Gortex metadata-only telemetry and SQLite audit/event lineage. No raw prompts, context, source, or responses by default. Arize Phoenix override: not selected for this phase for data-retention and dependency-minimization reasons.

**Key Metrics to Track:**

- Allowed, denied, failed, schema-rejected, semantic-rejected, and persisted invocations by provider/model/policy/scope identifiers.
- Claims by assertion kind and lifecycle: pending, confirmed, contradicted, rejected, stale, superseded.
- Invalid citation families: unknown, inactive, out-of-manifest, out-of-scope, stale revision, wrong predicate/kind.
- Retry deduplication conflicts and unexpected duplicate active claim count.
- Review latency and outcome distribution, without treating confirmation rate as truth quality by itself.
- AI-on/off deterministic digest mismatch count.
- Usage, latency, and bounded output sizes without raw content.

**Alert Thresholds:**

- Any deterministic digest mismatch, scope/protected-source canary leak, unauthorized dispatch, deterministic-row mutation attempt, or admitted invalid citation: immediate release/operation block.
- Any duplicate active claim ID, lost provenance field, invalid trusted-view inclusion, or failed required audit commit: page/disable claim writes.
- Schema/semantic rejection rate increases by more than 10 percentage points for the same dataset/version: investigate and suspend the changed model/prompt/schema combination.
- Stale/contradicted/rejected rate materially exceeds the accepted baseline: return the version to experimental-only and perform expert review.

**Smart Sampling Strategy:**

Review 100% of claims while Phase 8 remains experimental. If later governance allows reduced sampling, retain 100% review for new provider/model/prompt/context/schema versions, high-impact subjects, unresolved boundaries, conflicts, confidence extremes, policy near-limits, and changed evidence generations. Randomly sample the remainder, stratified by assertion kind and scope. Sampling never changes admission or trusted-view rules.

### Privacy and Audit Constraints

- Metrics and traces contain IDs, hashes, versions, counts, timing, and decisions only by default.
- Raw prompts, source context, provider responses, and reviewer-visible source are not copied into telemetry.
- Claim rationale and missing-context strings are bounded and scanned under the Phase 7 policy before persistence.
- Confidential audit file permissions, retention, encryption, and legal hold remain Phase 7 policy inputs, not defaults invented here.
